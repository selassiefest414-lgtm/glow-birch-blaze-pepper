# Rainbow DJ Lab: Academic Governance, Endowment, and Institutional Prestige Blueprint

Board discussion draft | Ras Tafari Inc. leadership | November 2026

Ras Tafari Inc. leadership, build Rainbow DJ Lab as a selective, accessible conservatory-style program for DJ performance and electronic music, rooted in the Black and queer lineages of Chicago house and the wider Great Lakes music community. Establish its reputation through compelling student work, paid master practitioners, independent assessment, and durable scholarships. Keep “Ivy League” as an internal quality benchmark, not a claim of affiliation or accreditation.

**Recommended sequence:** Launch a clearly labeled noncredit community-arts program after Wisconsin regulatory review; negotiate college-recognized learning separately; pursue ACCPAS if the Lab remains a community-arts school, or evaluate NASM if the Board authorizes a substantially larger professional postsecondary program. Keep the operating budget, equipment campaign, and permanent endowment separate. This draft takes Articles IX and XI of File #75598726 as described by leadership; the signed bylaws were not available for verification. Counsel should review the signed instrument before the Board adopts a charter or delegates authority.

## Pillar 1: Academic \& Industry Advisory Board

### Proposed charter for adoption under Article IX

1. **Purpose:** The Academic \& Industry Advisory Board advises Ras Tafari Inc.’s Board of Directors on academic quality, artistic direction, industry relevance, faculty capability, and accreditation readiness. If counsel determines that Article IX applies only to director committees, establish the advisory body by a separate Board resolution without delegating corporate powers. A Wisconsin nonprofit committee exercising Board authority under Wis. Stat. § 181.0825 must have at least three directors; outside experts should not occupy those seats ([Wisconsin Legislature](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/181/viii/0825)).
2. **Composition:** Appoint nine voting advisors: two Chicago house or Great Lakes DJ culture stewards; one producer with verified major credits, ideally a Grammy-winning producer who can commit to the work; one university music technology or electronic composition professor; one conservatory dean or former accreditation lead; one label, publishing, or artist-development executive; one touring DJ, venue programmer, or live-sound leader; one community arts or accessibility educator; and one learner or alumni representative. Until there are alumni, seat a Racine-area community learner representative. Seat the Academic Director and a designated corporate director as nonvoting liaisons.
3. **Appointment and meetings:** The corporate Board appoints members and the chair. Use staggered two-year terms, with a one-year initial term for four seats and a maximum of two consecutive full terms; use a one-year renewable learner appointment. Meet quarterly. A majority of filled voting seats constitutes a quorum; a majority of eligible votes cast approves recommendations, not corporate action. A recused member does not count toward the quorum for that item. Maintain minutes and a recommendation register.
4. **Annual work:** In Q1, review syllabi, outcomes and assessment evidence; in Q2, publish an internal industry and technology scan; in Q3, review de-identified capstone samples and a standards gap analysis; in Q4, submit a prioritized academic quality report with proposed actions, owners, costs, and measures. The corporate Board records each recommendation as accepted, deferred, or declined and assigns follow-up.
5. **Reserved authority:** Advisors cannot sign, hire, spend, accept gifts, negotiate binding terms, represent the Lab to regulators or accreditors, approve institutional policies, decide individual admissions or grades, or access identifiable student files in their advisory role. The Academic Director leads curriculum development and administers approved policies; the corporate Board retains institutional policy, budget, contracts, and accreditation decisions. No sponsor gets a reserved seat or veto over curriculum, procurement, or assessment.
6. **Conduct and liability:** Require an appointment letter, confidentiality, annual and event-specific conflict disclosures, documented recusals, and consent before publicizing an advisor’s affiliation. Advisors are not appointed as corporate directors, officers, or agents by virtue of this role; the charter is not a waiver of liability for their own conduct. Ask counsel and the insurer to confirm coverage, and document paid teaching or consulting separately at independently reviewed market rates.

### Make the advisory work visible without relying on celebrity

* **Recruitment:** Start with one Chicago house steward, one academic quality lead, and one Racine community educator as founding anchors. Give each candidate a specific first-year assignment before seeking permission to announce their name.
* **Institutional signature:** Establish an annual Rainbow Lab Jury and public showcase, an artist-led Chicago house oral history series with permission and agreed rights, and an annual academic outcomes brief. Credit and compensate culture bearers; publish student work only with informed consent and appropriate music clearances.

## Pillar 2: Accreditation, academic standards, and credit

### Set the accreditation route by what the Lab actually teaches

|Route|Where it fits|Gate before proceeding|
|-|-|-|
|**ACCPAS: community or precollegiate arts**|Community arts instruction can serve adults as well as youth and may include a completion certificate, but it does not lead to a degree or other postsecondary credential.|Confirm in writing whether the applicant is Rainbow DJ Lab or Ras Tafari Inc., how the operating-history rule applies, and which primary-accreditor requirements apply. An independent institution must have operated for at least three consecutive years, sustain regular academic-year or year-round offerings, maintain sufficient enrollment, and meet applicable legal requirements ([ACCPAS Handbook, I and Appendix I.A](https://accpas.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/02/ACCPAS-2026-Handbook-Final-02-20-2026.pdf)).|
|**NASM: professional postsecondary non-degree program**|A coherent professional music program, not a short DJ course or vendor training badge. NASM’s Commission on Accreditation reviews independent postsecondary music schools of this type ([NASM institutional membership](https://nasm.arts-accredit.org/accreditation/general-information/institutional-membership/)).|NASM currently requires at least one eligible program of 30 undergraduate semester hours or equivalent, or 15 graduate semester hours or equivalent. At least one qualifying program must have been completed by students, have at least three enrolled, and be progressing students toward completion; separately, at least 15 students must be in residence in eligible curricula for membership consideration. Independent institutions need at least three consecutive years of operation. Budget for the applicable CPA audit and verify music-technology, admission, finance, and freestanding-institution standards with NASM ([NASM Handbook, I.C, II.B–C, XVIII–XIX, XXI and Appendix I.H](https://nasm.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/02/M-2025-26-Handbook-Final-02-24-2026.pdf)).|
|**College-issued credit: independent of either pursuit**|A college can evaluate prior learning or, under a separate agreement, offer an approved credit-bearing course. Rainbow does not award college credit in either arrangement.|Obtain a receiving institution’s written approval and verify the arrangement with Wisconsin and any relevant accreditor before marketing it. Gateway Technical College publishes a faculty-evaluated prior learning process; UW–Parkside’s music program is another exploratory conversation, not an existing partnership ([Gateway Technical College](https://www.gtc.edu/admissions/credit-prior-learning); [UW–Parkside music catalog](https://catalog.uwp.edu/programs/music/)).|

**Do not treat ACCPAS as a prerequisite or automatic bridge to NASM.** A shift from community arts to vocational or postsecondary education changes the regulatory and institutional analysis. Wisconsin’s avocational exemption concerns recreational instruction with no vocational objective; public charity status alone does not settle approval. Before marketing or enrolling students, send Wisconsin DSPS’s Educational Approval Program the actual audience, calendar, curriculum, tuition, delivery mode, career claims, credential language, and any college arrangement; request a written approval or exemption determination ([Wisconsin DSPS exemptions](https://dsps.wi.gov/eap-home-page/online-institutions-and-exempt-schools/); [new-school approval](https://dsps.wi.gov/eap-home-page/obtaining-approval/)).

**Governance gate: the current three-director structure may need to grow.** For a nonprofit freestanding applicant subject to ACCPAS Appendix I.A or NASM XXI.1.D, plan for a legal governing board of at least five, with the chair and a majority of the other voting members free of the specified financial interests, and a chief executive who is not the chair. ACCPAS Appendix I.A additionally calls for a paid, full-time chief executive and an independent annual CPA audit completed within 180 days of fiscal year-end. NASM requires an employee chief executive whose primary responsibility is to the institution, and its financial standards also require a timely annual independent audit for privately supported institutions. The advisory board cannot satisfy the legal governing-board requirement. Obtain the accreditor’s scope determination, then have counsel map any needed bylaw changes and recruit independent directors before application ([ACCPAS Handbook, Appendix I.A](https://accpas.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/02/ACCPAS-2026-Handbook-Final-02-20-2026.pdf); [NASM Handbook, II.C and XXI.1.D](https://nasm.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/02/M-2025-26-Handbook-Final-02-24-2026.pdf)).

### Multi-year readiness plan

1. **Year 1 | Establish the record:** Confirm state status, publish a controlled catalog, and run a proposed 120 instructional-hour noncredit community-arts certificate: DJ Performance and Musicianship (36), Electronic Production and Critical Listening (36), House Lineage, Rights and Artist Practice (24), and Capstone Studio (24). Log contact hours and independent practice separately. Preserve sample work, assessor scores, policy versions, and evidence of changes made after each cohort.
2. **Year 2 | Prove repeatability:** Run continuously scheduled cohorts; commission an external curriculum and accessibility review; test scoring consistency and start college mapping. Request a pre-application scope conversation with ACCPAS or NASM, not public “candidate” status. Identify the applicant entity, confirm the freestanding standards and operating-history rules, and budget for governance expansion, executive staffing and audits if applicable.
3. **Year 3 | Make a Board decision:** Complete a gap analysis and mock visit. Choose the community route unless documented demand, financing, state authority, suitable faculty, a broader music curriculum, and capacity for the NASM enrollment and graduate evidence justify the professional route. For NASM, develop an actual postsecondary curriculum with aural skills, rhythm and form, music history, creative and collaborative practice, technology and audio systems, and juried work; do not convert pilot hours into invented academic credits.
4. **Years 4–5 and beyond | Seek external review only when eligible:** For ACCPAS, confirm timing with the national office, submit the application notice, prepare the self-study, host evaluators, respond to the report if warranted, and await the official action letter. For NASM, submit the Notice of Intention at least two years ahead for preferred scheduling and no later than one year before the visit under current procedures; complete the required self-study, portfolio, visit, and Commission review. A new professional program’s first graduates, enrollment, or visit scheduling could push independent NASM accreditation beyond Year 5. Disclose only verified status; neither application nor a visit is accreditation ([ACCPAS review process](https://accpas.arts-accredit.org/accreditation/general-information/overview/); [NASM Procedures for Institutions](https://nasm.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/07/M-2025-Procedures-for-Institutions-Final-06-01-2026.pdf)).

### Adopt the academic policies before the first cohort

* **Catalog and student policy:** Publish admissions and placement, prerequisites, skills and equipment expectations, schedule and instructional hours, progression and attendance, completion and reassessment, withdrawal and refunds, full tuition and fees, scholarship criteria, accessibility accommodations, conduct and safeguarding, grievances and independent grade appeals, and records and disclosures. Keep a closure and record-custody plan. Publish the faculty roster and distinguish regular faculty from guests. These are practical controls informed by both handbooks, not a claim that one accreditor prescribes Rainbow’s particular hour count or rubric ([ACCPAS Handbook](https://accpas.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/02/ACCPAS-2026-Handbook-Final-02-20-2026.pdf); [NASM Handbook](https://nasm.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/02/M-2025-26-Handbook-Final-02-24-2026.pdf)).
* **Assessment evidence:** Use an entry diagnostic, a midpoint critique, and an exit portfolio. Require a 20-minute recorded set, an original production with session or stems and a stereo export, annotated track and rights logs, and a short oral defense. Use two calibrated assessors, at least one independent of the course where practicable; refer a pass/fail disagreement or a gap of more than one rubric level to an independent third assessor. Offer one documented reassessment and a separate appeal.
* **Institutional review:** Each term, record enrollment, withdrawals, completion, capstone pass rate, assessor agreement, and access to scholarships and lab hours, using explicit denominators. Review equity and access without publishing identifiable small groups. Annually, document one improvement for each material gap and verify its effect the following year.

|Capstone criterion|Weight|Level 3: proficient|
|-|-:|-|
|Performance technique|25%|Phrasing, beat alignment, transitions and gain staging serve the set; student recovers from a cue or routing issue without stopping.|
|Musicianship and programming|20%|Track choices and sequencing demonstrate deliberate energy, musical continuity and stylistic understanding.|
|Production and listening|25%|Original work has coherent arrangement and controlled balance; session is organized and exports reproduce the intended mix.|
|Context, credits and rights|15%|Places the work in a credible lineage, credits contributors and sources, and identifies what can and cannot be shared publicly.|
|Professional practice and safety|15%|Completes setup and teardown, follows level and hearing protocols, maintains usable backups and communicates a clear handoff.|

Use a four-level scale: **4** = independent, consistent and adaptable; **3** = independent at stated proficiency; **2** = inconsistent or needs prompting; **1** = insufficient or unsafe; missing work is **0**. Calculate the weighted score as the sum of each weight times the criterion rating divided by four. Require at least 75/100, no criterion below 2, and at least 3 in both rights and professional practice; a critical safety incident or prohibited use of material bars completion pending remediation.

### Give the certificate evidentiary weight without overstating it

* **Certificate face:** “Rainbow DJ Lab Certificate of Completion: DJ Performance \& Electronic Music Foundations. Issued by Ras Tafari Inc. Noncredit; no college credit awarded by Rainbow DJ Lab. Any award of academic credit is determined by the receiving institution.” Add learner name, award date, unique ID, authorized signature, and a privacy-respecting verification method.
* **Companion record:** Issue a secure record with course codes, syllabus versions, delivery mode, verified instructional hours, assessed outcomes and ratings, award criteria, and consent-based portfolio access. Retain the minimum official award record and approved retention rules for underlying evidence; make the record reproducible if a student returns years later.
* **Recognition route:** Map assessed work to a specific college course, share de-identified exemplars, and ask its registrar and faculty for a written prior learning or articulation pathway. The agreement should state the course equivalency, credit amount and issuer, assessment and residency conditions, effective dates, renewal rules, and exact public wording. For a jointly delivered course, the college should own academic approval, credit, and its transcript. An ACE evaluation can be considered when scale warrants it; an ACE recommendation is not a promise of acceptance ([ACE National Guide](https://www.acenet.edu/National-Guide/Pages/CPL-Policies.aspx)).

## Pillar 3: Permanent endowment and financial sustainability

### Create three distinct funding pools

* **Current operations and reserves:** Use unrestricted contributions, earned revenue, operating grants and current-use scholarship gifts according to their terms. Develop a separate reserve target of six months of core expenses before taking on a major permanent lease or expanding fixed payroll.
* **Capital and replacements:** Use equipment gifts and cash for a defined fit-out, repairs, replacement, licenses and accessibility. Do not count donated desks, turntables or licenses as permanent endowment principal.
* **Permanent endowment:** Begin with one pooled Rainbow DJ Lab Access Endowment with donor-restricted perpetual gifts and a broad educational purpose, such as need-based access and related student support. Keep separate accounting for each donor restriction within any pooled account. A board-designated quasi-endowment remains distinct from donor-restricted endowment and is not marketed as the same thing ([Wis. Stat. § 112.11](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/112/11)).

### Five-year campaign

Use **$1 million in cumulative collected permanent gifts** as a working goal, subject to feasibility, not a forecast. Keep revocable legacy intentions, unpaid pledges, in-kind gifts, and current-use sponsorships outside this cash target. At a 4% illustrative payout, a $1 million valuation base supports about $40,000 annually before any adjustment; a $25,000 annual scholarship objective needs roughly $625,000 at the same rate. These are illustrations, not guaranteed distributions, and the campaign must not fund recurring commitments in advance of approved grants.

|Year|New collected gifts|Cumulative collected gifts|Development priority|
|-|-:|-:|-|
|1|$50,000|$50,000|Interview at least 15 prospects; adopt gift agreements and investment policy; secure leadership seed gifts in a quiet phase.|
|2|$100,000|$150,000|Seek founding major donors and one challenge gift; prepare counsel-reviewed bequest language and launch a legacy society.|
|3|$200,000|$350,000|Solicit multi-year major gifts and paid-in matching gifts; approach AlphaTheta and Ableton separately about current-use gear, licenses or cash support.|
|4|$275,000|$625,000|Seek named funds only above a Board-set minimum tied to meaningful payout; broaden the local and alumni donor base and review concentrations.|
|5|$375,000|$1,000,000|Close lead gifts, publish a stewardship report, validate the gift pipeline and budget only Board-approved distributions.|

* **Campaign discipline:** Maintain a prospect pipeline, signed commitments and collections as separate measures. Set an initial target of qualified asks totaling three times the next year’s cash goal; reset the campaign if the quiet phase cannot demonstrate lead-gift capacity. Fund development costs and pilot operations outside permanent gifts unless a reviewed instrument expressly allows a different treatment.
* **Gift acceptance and planned giving:** Start with cash, liquid publicly traded securities under a written liquidation policy, bequests, and beneficiary designations. Require counsel and CPA review for real estate, privately held interests, restricted or illiquid assets, or life-income arrangements. Track donor instructions and gift acknowledgments, confirm Wisconsin solicitation registration or exemption before campaign launch, and monitor public support as donor concentration changes ([IRS gift substantiation and disclosures](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-organizations-substantiation-and-disclosure-requirements); [Wisconsin DFI charity FAQ](https://dfi.wi.gov/Pages/BusinessServices/CharitableProfessionalOrganizations/CharitableOrganizationsFAQ.aspx); [IRS tax-exempt organization overview](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/stay-exempt/understanding-key-topics)).
* **Sponsor boundaries:** Offer simple name/logo recognition and impact reporting, not endorsement, sales calls to action, student data, grades, admissions preferences, or mandatory purchasing. Separately value and review advertising or other substantial return benefits and any potential unrelated business income. Treat these brands as prospects, not partners or endorsements; count only money explicitly accepted into the endowment as permanent gifts ([IRS sponsorship guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/advertising-or-qualified-sponsorship-payments)).

### Recharter Finance without creating an oversight gap

1. **Structure:** Given the described three-director Board, retain budget, operating controls, audit/CPA oversight and Form 990 accountability. Either have the full Board initially act on endowment matters with an advisory working group, or recharter Finance as a **Finance, Endowment \& Investment Committee** with the three directors if counsel confirms authority. When the Board has more capacity, spin out a dedicated **Endowment \& Investment Committee** while retaining a separate finance and audit function. Investment, advancement and accounting specialists can advise without a vote or signing authority ([Wis. Stat. § 181.0825](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/181/viii/0825)).
2. **Mandate:** Recommend asset allocation, benchmarks, authorized investments, risk limits, rebalancing, custody, manager selection, gift acceptance, and annual appropriations. Board approval remains required for the investment policy, manager contract, material or nonstandard gifts, and appropriation. Start with a diversified, liquid portfolio suited to a small fund, with independent custody and no speculative or illiquid holdings without a separate Board decision. Document the scope of any delegation and review the agent periodically ([Wis. Stat. § 112.11(3), (5)](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/112/11)).
3. **Spending policy:** Propose up to 4% of the trailing 12 quarter-end market values once a fund has that history and can sustain a meaningful distribution. Default to no spending before that point or from underwater funds unless specifically approved after a written prudence review and gift-term check. Minute the seven relevant statutory considerations: duration and preservation, institutional and fund purposes, economic conditions, inflation or deflation, expected total return, other institutional resources, and investment policy. This is a proposed ceiling, not a legal safe harbor or automatic payout ([Wis. Stat. § 112.11(4)](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/112/11)).
4. **Article XI controls:** Preserve existing signatory tiers. Separate authorization, execution, and monthly reconciliation; where staffing overlaps, require independent director review. Maintain a donor restriction register and reconcile custody to the general ledger. Report quarterly on collected gifts versus pledges, investment returns net of fees, restrictions, underwater funds, payouts, and reserve coverage. Ask the CPA to verify applicable accounting and disclosures, and counsel to review any change to a donor restriction before action ([Wis. Stat. § 112.11](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/112/11)).

### Procure for assessed outcomes, not for the equipment list alone

Design the pilot for **12 learners**, with three two-person practice booths and six individual production stations. Stagger the room rotations so every learner gets supervised hands-on time, and schedule bookable practice outside class. These are Rainbow specifications, not accreditor-mandated brands.

### Hire master practitioners who can teach

* **Academic Director:** Prefer a relevant graduate degree or documented equivalent, program leadership, curriculum and assessment experience, plus credible artistic or production practice. Own faculty files, catalog control, complaints routing, quality review and self-study.
* **Core faculty:** Use a relevant degree plus verified practice for applied work, or document equivalent preparation through substantial professional credits, sustained practice, references, pedagogical evidence and a panel justification by course. Prefer a relevant graduate degree or strong scholarly equivalent for history, theory and pedagogy. Do not let fame substitute for teaching ability; NASM’s practitioner guidance discusses experience, education and expertise at least equivalent to a pertinent master’s degree when a highly qualified practitioner lacks the conventional degree ([NASM Handbook, II.E](https://nasm.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/02/M-2025-26-Handbook-Final-02-24-2026.pdf); [ACCPAS Handbook, II.E](https://accpas.arts-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/02/ACCPAS-2026-Handbook-Final-02-20-2026.pdf)).
* **Hiring rubric:** Score applied or scholarly mastery (30%), a paid teaching audition with an accessible micro-lesson and feedback on novice work (30%), assessment calibration (20%), inclusive and culturally grounded practice (10%), and reliability and safety (10%). Require 75/100 and at least 70% of the points in teaching and assessment individually. Verify credits, references, credentials, and safeguarding checks where applicable before appointments.
* **Faculty model and retention:** Start with an Academic Director, lead performance instructor, lead production instructor, and lab technician; contract specialists for history and rights. Allocate clear instructional, preparation and assessment time. Offer renewable two- or three-year **Distinguished Practitioner** or **Artist-in-Residence** appointments, paid preparation and mentoring, studio access, flexible scheduling around touring, and opportunities to design residencies. Use “tenure” only if the institution later adopts and funds a formal tenure system. Separate guest artists from faculty of record; use annual peer observation, learner feedback, work samples and documented improvement in renewal decisions.

## Board action in the first 90 days

1. **Days 1–30 | Board Chair, Secretary and counsel:** Verify the signed Articles IX and XI, adopt the advisory charter and reserved-powers schedule, appoint an Academic Director or interim lead, and open the Wisconsin scope review. No external accreditation or transfer claims.
2. **Days 31–60 | Academic Director and Treasurer:** Finalize the pilot catalog, credential language, rubric, records policy and faculty hiring packet. Prepare a course-mapping sample for Gateway and UW–Parkside, a first-year operating and capital budget, and draft gift acceptance, investment and spending policies for Board review.
3. **Days 61–90 | Corporate Board:** Review written regulatory status, launch funding, faculty and facilities readiness, and the student protections package. Authorize enrollment only after the applicable state requirements are met and the cohort can be fully delivered. Consider the endowment quiet phase separately, after counsel and the CPA clear gift documents, solicitation status, and controls.

In Year 1, judge success by the quality and consistency of assessed student work, reliable access to instruction and equipment, and financial controls that withstand independent review. Those are the foundations for the program’s reputation, whether the Lab ultimately remains a distinguished community arts school or grows into a professional postsecondary institution.
